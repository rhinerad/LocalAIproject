# Opening sequence — rewritten shot lists

Scene 000, clips `00A` through `00O`. **Shot lists only.** Camera, cast, identity, lighting, dialogue timing, audio and prohibitions are unchanged — drop these blocks in place of the `SHOT LIST` section in column U.

Staging read from `boundary_narrator_close_v3.png` and `boundary_entry_start_v2.png`: bare walls, **one chair far screen-left**, **service window centre-right with the receptionist framed inside it, hands folded on the sill**, **open doorway far screen-right with a lit corridor behind it**. The receptionist's rest pose is hands folded, eyes level, mouth closed. Every beat below returns her to that pose so the model has something to come back to.

## Two rules applied to every beat

**Business is anchored to a phrase, not to a stopwatch.** Each block starts where a phrase of narration starts. A beat that begins mid-phrase reads as an accident; one that begins on a phrase reads as direction.

**Mouths are locked shut, explicitly, in every block.** The muttering is the model inventing speech for anyone whose face is visible while audio is playing. It will not stop because the prompt says it once at the bottom — it has to be denied in the same block that gives the person something to do, because that is where the model decides what their face is doing.

[14-15 s] Held. The Narrator finishes and stays still. The receptionist is back in the exact rest pose she started in. Nothing else moves.

## `00D` — 12 s, narration 1–5 s, dual anchor

Narration covers five seconds of a twelve-second clip. The remaining seven are pure action and were previously undirected — this is the clip where the men's business has to carry the shot on its own.

> "If there's one chair in a room and two people."

```
SHOT LIST
[0-1 s] Held on the empty public room. The receptionist is folded in the window. The doorway at screen-right is empty.
[1-2.5 s] "If there's one chair in a room" - Male Visitor One steps through the doorway at screen-right and walks into the public room. He does not hesitate or look around. The receptionist unfolds her hands as she registers him.
[2.5-3.5 s] Male Visitor One reaches the service window and stops squarely in front of it.
[3.5-5 s] "and two people" - Male Visitor Two steps through the same doorway, exactly one second behind, and stops a pace behind Visitor One, forming a short queue. He faces forward and waits. He does not tap the first man's shoulder or lean around him.
[5-7 s] Visitor One slides a form across the sill. The receptionist stamps it once, slides it back, and nods a single small nod. No words pass between them. Visitor One steps away from the window toward the chair at screen-left.

[9-11 s] Visitor Two crosses to the chair and stops beside Visitor One. Both men now stand on the public side, the single chair between them, and turn to face each other for the first time.

Nobody speaks in this shot. Every mouth in frame is completely closed at all times: no muttering, no mouthed words, no lip movement, no silent conversation between the two men, no talking to the receptionist. The check-in is transacted entirely through the form, the stamp and the nod.

## `00D2` — 10 s, narration 1–9 s, dual anchor

> "Both people insist that the other person takes the seat. / Even if one does take it, the other goes without."

The gesture loop needs a stated count. Left open, the model either does it once and stalls or loops until the bodies blur.

```
SHOT LIST
[0-1 s] Held. Both men stand facing each other beside the single chair. The receptionist watches from behind the window, hands folded, mouth closed.
[1-3 s] "Both people insist" - Visitor One extends an open palm toward the chair, offering it. Visitor Two answers by raising both hands slightly and shaking his head once.
[3-5 s] "that the other person takes the seat" - Visitor Two returns the offer, gesturing to the chair with an open palm. Visitor One declines with a small bow of the head and a hand to his own chest. Exactly two offers have now been made, one each.
[5-7 s] A third and final exchange, smaller and more tired than the first two: Visitor One gestures again, Visitor Two hesitates. Their shoulders drop. This is the moment the deadlock breaks.
[7-9 s] "Even if one does take it, the other goes without" - Visitor Two gestures once more toward the chair and holds it. Visitor One yields, steps in front of the chair, and sits. Visitor Two remains standing beside him, hands returning to his sides.
[9-10 s] Held. One man seated, one man standing beside him, the receptionist still folded behind the window. This is the closing frame.

There are exactly three offer-and-refusal exchanges in this shot, no more. Nobody speaks. Every mouth in frame is completely closed at all times: no muttering, no mouthed words, no lip movement, no polite conversation, no "after you" being mouthed. ## `00E` — 8 s, narration 0.5–7.8 s, dual anchor

> "The sitting now looks up at the standing, / and the standing looks down at the sitting. / Both people are now filled with debilitating anxiety."

The script asks for a shot-reverse-shot feeling inside one unbroken take. That means the focus shifts, not the camera.

```
SHOT LIST
[0-0.5 s] Held. The seated man and the standing man beside the chair, both still.
[0.5-3 s] "The sitting now looks up at the standing" - focus rests on the seated man. He raises his eyes to the standing man's face. His head lifts only slightly; the movement is in the eyes.
[3-5.5 s] "and the standing looks down at the sitting" - focus shifts to the standing man without the camera moving. He lowers his eyes to the seated man. Their eyes meet.
[5.5-7.8 s] "Both people are now filled with debilitating anxiety" - neither breaks the look. The seated man's hands close on his knees. The standing man's hands close at his sides. Both hold the eye contact past the point of comfort.
[7.8-8 s] Held on the locked look. This is the closing frame.

The focus shifts between the two men; the camera does not move, cut, or change angle. Nobody speaks. Both mouths are completely closed at all times: no muttering, no mouthed words, no lip movement, no about-to-speak mouth openings.
```

## `00F` — 15 s, narration 1–14 s, single anchor

The seated man's mouth is completely closed in every frame - the pressed lips are the point. No muttering, no mouthed words, no lip movement, no words he almost says.
