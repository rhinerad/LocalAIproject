PART 0 — SOURCE NARRATIVE
====================================================================
Use this narrative as the authoritative source for every clip's dialogue, action, tone, and timing. Derive each of the 102 clips' VIDEO_PROMPT text from the relevant beat below — do not invent story content that isn't grounded in this text.

The amber digital text in the lower-right corner of the frame reads AUG 09 1998, glowing softly against a standard 4:3 cathode-ray frame filled with the low-grade warmth of analog tape hiss. The video opens in a cramped, cluttered 1990s apartment living room that has been unceremoniously converted into a makeshift casting studio. A single piece of masking tape forms an "X" on the worn carpet. Standing directly on the mark is Leo, an actor in his late 20s wearing a faded olive-green overshirt over a gray T-shirt, clutching a stack of dog-eared script pages for a sitcom pilot called Wacky Roommates.

For the first two minutes and forty seconds, the project plays entirely straight as a painful, mundane self-tape. Leo looks into the lens of the tripod-mounted camcorder with a practiced, overly enthusiastic grin, launching into a series of deeply unfunny comedic beats and delivered lines. He pauses awkwardly for non-existent laughter, flubs his dialogue, drops his pages, and mutters self-deprecating remarks under his breath before resetting. The humor is dry, grounded, and entirely unintentional.

The tone shifts abruptly when Leo finishes his final take, walks up to the camera to review the footage, and attempts to lift it off the cheap tripod. The device refuses to budge. What begins as a minor annoyance quickly escalates into a frantic physical struggle. Leo grips the camera body with both hands, plants his sneaker against a tripod leg, and yanks backward with all his weight, his face straining and reddening as the internal motor whirs and strains. With one violent, sudden jerk, the mount shears free.

The screen immediately fractures into a blur of violent motion and blinding exposure. The apartment walls stretch, warp, and smear into a sickening yellow streak of light and analog static before plunging into absolute dark.

When the image resolves, the camcorder is lying sideways on a damp, stained brown-beige patterned office carpet. A relentless, high-decibel fluorescent hum fills the audio track, vibrating like an electric wasp nest. Leo groans, his hand entering the frame to right the camera. Raising it to chest height, he stumbles to his feet, finding himself trapped in an endless, windowless labyrinth of sickly yellow patterned wallpaper and low acoustic ceiling tiles. This is Level 0.

For the next several minutes, the film adopts a slow-burn, psychological dread. The camera tracks Leo's steady, paranoid wandering through infinite identical hallways and doorless openings. The only sounds are his heavy breathing, the wet squelch of carpet beneath his sneakers, and the oppressive drone of the lights. Seeking to prove the architecture is sane, Leo pulls a pen from his pocket and scratches a distinct arrow onto a yellow wall before exploring a complex pattern of turns. When he loops back to the same coordinate minutes later, the pen mark has inexplicably inverted, pointing backward. The environment is actively lying to him.

A hard cut breaks the monotony. Leo rounds a corner into a subtly warped corridor and stops dead, his breath catching in his throat. Half-melted directly into the yellow wallpaper like wax in a furnace are ordinary relics of the 1980s: an old, beat-up skateboard, a wooden electric guitar with rusted strings, and a plaid flannel shirt are fused permanently into the wall partitions. He reaches out a trembling hand, his fingertips brushing the guitar wood — it feels simultaneously real and impossible, welded into the architecture by some unknown thermodynamic anomaly.

Another hard cut expands the scale into architectural claustrophobia. Leo stumbles into a vast, open-plan office expanse punctuated by heavy concrete industrial pillars stretching into a yellow haze. The sightlines are unnervingly long, and distant, phantom whispers echo across the empty space ("Are you lost?", "Don't go that way"), driving him into a brisk, terrified jog through the shadows.

A third hard cut introduces a bizarre, surreal spatial failure: an indoor urban park enclosed entirely beneath low concrete and fluorescent grid ceilings. The artificial illumination here is a sickly emerald green. The trunks of the artificial trees glow with an aggressive sun-orange bark, while surrounding foliage exhibits inverted, chemically wrong color palettes. Leo steps gingerly along a false gravel path, whispering disbelievingly into the microphone as the ozone-heavy air fills his lungs.

Following this, after nearly eight hours of continuous, exhausting wandering, Leo's physical endurance breaks. Rounding a corner into a narrow utility passage, he discovers a shallow puddle of clear mystery water resting on the damp carpet. Overcome with immense physical relief, he whispers, "Water," and drops the camcorder onto the floor. The camera rests at a low, upward angle, capturing Leo as he kneels in the dirt, frantically washing his sweat-soaked face and scooping handfuls of the liquid to his mouth. His loud, desperate gulps and splashing hands drown out the fluorescent hum for a brief, human moment before he retrieves the camera and presses onward.

The narrative climax hits at the twelve-minute mark. Leo rounds a corner and pans down a long, narrow corridor. Following a brief burst of analog static, the frame clears to reveal a very tall, extremely thin humanoid silhouette standing completely motionless in the middle distance under a buzzing fixture. It is the Entity: charcoal-black surface, long limbs hanging past its knees, and an oversized, featureless head. Leo gasps and freezes, bracing for an attack that never comes.

Instead, the horror pivots into an extraordinary sequence of absurdist physical comedy. Leo, paralyzed by sheer confusion, raises his hand and gives a timid, nervous wave. Across the corridor, the nine-foot entity slowly mirrors the gesture. Leo tests it: he steps left, it steps left; he steps right, it steps right. He adopts an awkward balancing pose on one leg, and the impossible creature copies it with eerie precision. Over the next three minutes, they engage in a silent, deadpan mime routine — an exaggerated shoulder shimmy, a clumsy dance step, and a theatrical stage bow that the entity attempts with terrifying, non-violent flexibility. Leo stares into the lens, deadpan and exhausted, muttering, "This is somehow worse than the audition."

The comedy dissolves into profound dread as Leo decides to test the boundaries. In the final minutes, he stands completely upright, waiting. The entity stops mirroring, its posture locking. Leo takes a slow step closer and asks, "You are doing that on purpose, right?"

The entity's head lifts unprompted. The silence stretches. Then, in a crisp, ordinary, entirely normal and slightly goofy human voice, the entity speaks for the first time:

"What's weird?"

Leo flinches violently, nearly dropping the camera in utter disbelief. He stares at the featureless face, whispering, "You... can talk?"

Before he can process the response, the background audio changes. A faint, distant sound of frantic yelling begins echoing down the hallway. Over the span of forty-five agonizing seconds, the noise builds: by the thirty-second mark, a panicked human voice is unmistakably screaming "Get out! Get out!" over and over, accompanied by the thunderous, chaotic clatter of heavy running footsteps closing in at terrifying speed.

Both Leo and the tall entity snap their heads downward toward the floor grating where the screaming is originating. For a single, frozen second, they share a synchronized look of pure, unadulterated terror.

At the forty-five-second mark, without a single word exchanged, both the entity and the actor turn and break into a blind sprint together, fleeing down the corridor in opposite directions or side-by-side into the gloom. The camera shakes with violent, breathless handheld motion blur. The screaming and deafening footsteps peak to an unbearable, wall-shaking roar right at the frame edge — and the video cuts out instantly to black.
